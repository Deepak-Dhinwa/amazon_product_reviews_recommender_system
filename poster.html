Office Products Recommender

With sparse star ratings, a small matrix factorization recommends better than a content model that barely sees the user.

Surprise SVDStreamlitRecall@10

Question

  • Given only star ratings on Amazon office products, what should this shopper see next?
  • Most users review a handful of items, so the matrix is mostly empty.

Method

  • Collaborative filtering with Surprise SVD on the filtered rating matrix.
  • Compared with truncated SVD, a hybrid, and content baselines.
  • The app hides items the user has already reviewed and shows the top 10.

Evidence

0.84SVD RMSE
0.94SVD Recall@10
10recommendations shown

What the result actually says

  • The served model is Surprise SVD. On the filtered matrix it sits near RMSE 0.84 and Recall@10 0.94.
  • A known user in the file is AN2FZ6SBNYDDQ. The page drops items that user already reviewed and shows ten.
  • The filtered table is about 370,000 reviews from the Amazon office-products 5-core set: reviewer, item, and star rating.

What it does not show

  • Recall@10 this high is on a sparse, filtered matrix. It is not a promise about a cold-start shopper with no ratings.
  • The deep model and the hybrid were worse here, so they are documented and not what the app loads.

Beside the other attempts

  • Truncated SVD from scikit-learn: RMSE about 4.41. The rating scale is 1–5, so that model is not usable.
  • A popularity-plus-SVD hybrid: Recall@10 about 0.09.
  • The neural recommender: validation Recall@10 about 0.024.

Stack

Surprise SVD, saved as svd_model.pkl, served by Streamlit. The environment is amazon_rec_sys_env.
